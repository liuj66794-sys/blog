### Passage C（第 11—15 题）

①People using happy emojis (表情符号) may seem cheerful—but are they really happy? Researchers from the University of Tokyo wanted to investigate whether “display rules”—“socially appropriate facial expressions in a given situation”—existed in communications with emojis. The study saw about 1,289 volunteers from Japan using emojis in response to online conversations. The participants, most of whom were female and aged 11 to 26 years, self-reported the intensity of emotional expressions.

②Results suggested that happy emojis were often used to mask negative emotions and “manage the expression” to make a message seem more positive. Negative emojis, such as any of the sad face emojis, were only used when the participants felt very strong negative feelings.

③Experts found that people were most likely to use positive emojis when they were feeling negative emotions or when talking to people of higher status—but they were also less likely to express emotions through emojis toward this group of people. People were more likely to use accurate emojis to express their feelings when talking to close friends.

④“As online socializing becomes more popular, people have become accustomed to modifying their expressions and examining the appropriateness of their communication,” Moyu Liu, an emotional behavior expert at the University of Tokyo who led the research, said. “However, I realized that this may lead us to lose touch with our real emotions.”

1. What did researchers from the University of Tokyo want to find out?
A. How to use emojis to express our emotions properly.
B. When to make facial expressions socially appropriate.
C. If the display rules of facial expressions existed in communicating with emojis.
D. Whether happy emojis were used to mask negative emotions in communications.
2. What do we know about the participants of the research?
A. They were mostly female.
B. They were in their early twenties.
C. They were asked to report with emojis.
D. They were selected from the University of Tokyo.
3. When the participants had very strong negative emotions, they ______
A. used happy emojis
B. masked their feelings
C. modified their responses
D. chose negative emojis
4. People were more likely to use accurate emojis when ______.
A. they felt a little sad
B. they chatted with close friends
C. they needed to make a message more positive
D. they communicated with people of higher status
5. According to Moyu Liu, what might be the negative influence of this trend?
A. We will be out of touch with our real friends.
B. We will fail to manage our facial expressions.
C. We will spend too much time socializing online.
D. We will lose the ability to express our true feelings.

## 第二部分 · 五选五 （第 16—20 题 · 建议 10 分钟）

Much of the work in today’s world is accomplished（完成）in teams. Most people believe the best way to build a great team is to gather a group of the most talented individuals. __16__ Companies spend millions hiring top business people. Is their money well spent?

__17__ They focused on football, basketball and baseball. The results are mixed. For football and basketball, adding talented players to a team proves a good method, but only up to the point where 70% of the players are top talent; above that level, the team’s performance begins to decline. Interestingly, this trend isn’t evident in baseball, where additional individual talent keeps improving the team’s performance.

To explain this phenomenon, the researchers explored the degree to which a good performance by a team requires its members to coordinate（协调）their actions. __18__ In baseball, the performance of individual players is less dependent on teammates. They conclude that when task interdependence is high, team performance will suffer when there is too much talent, while individual talent will have positive effects on team performance when task interdependence is lower. If a basketball star is, for example, trying to gain a high personal point total, he may take a shot himself when it would be better to pass the ball to a teammate, affecting the team’s performance. Young children learning to play team sports are often told, “There is no I in TEAM.”

__19__ Another possibility is that when there is a lot of talent on a team, some players may make less effort. Just as in a game of tug-of-war（拔河比赛）, whenever a person is added, everyone else pulls the rope with less force.

__20__ An A-team may require a balance—not just A players, but a few generous B players as well.

A. Sports team owners spend millions of dollars attracting top talent.
B. Stars apparently do not follow this basic principle of sportsmanship.
C. Several recent studies examined the role of talent in the sports world.
D. Building up a dream team is more complex than simply hiring the best talent.
E. This task interdependence distinguishes baseball from football and basketball.

Jet airplanes and big trucks didn’t exist yet. __26__ January 26, Billy and three other children had died. Twenty more were __27__. Nome’s town officials came up with a(n) __28__. ## 第四部分 · 语法填空 （第 36—45 题 · 建议 10 分钟）

It was December 30th. Anna and Alex __36__ (be) helping Mom make banana cakes. They cut up the bananas, put the eggs and sugar in the flour, __37__ (pour) the milk in and mixed them all up.

When they were almost done, Anna asked, “Mom, __38__ are we making so many cakes?”

“I want to take some over to Mrs. Brown across the street,” said Mom. “Her husband died of a serious __39__ (ill) a few days ago. She only has her dog Wangwang in the house.”

Mom put the cakes in the oven and the children helped clean up the mess. The cakes were finally done. They all waited patiently until the cakes cooled down. Then they put some on a plate and headed to the opposite house.

When Mrs. Brown opened the door, Anna said, “Mrs. Brown, we have brought you some banana cakes.”

“Thank you. They smell delicious,” Mrs. Brown said. “Wangwang and I are feeling a little lonely these days. It’s kind __40__ you to come.”

“Would you like to join us tomorrow for New Year’s dinner?” asked Mom. “Take Wangwang, too.”

“Well, I’m not sure,” said Mrs. Brown.

“Please say yes,” said Alex. “Come to our house, and it will be great fun,” said Anna.

Anna was right. They never had so pleasant __41__ dinner. Mrs. Brown thanked them for __42__ (invite) Wangwang and her, __43__ (say) she felt better. __44__ (happy) was doubled when __45__ (shared).

::: details 本节答案与解析（第 36—45 题）

答案速查（本节答案与解析为笔记整理时补充的参考答案）：

| 36 | **were** | 37 | **poured** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 38 | **why** | 39 | **illness** |
| 40 | **of** | 41 | **a** |
| 42 | **inviting** | 43 | **saying** |
| 44 | **Happiness** | 45 | **shared** |

**逐题解析**

**36. 答案 were**　December 30th 的过去进行时用 were helping。

**37. 答案 poured**　与 cut、put、mixed 并列，应用过去式 poured。

**38. 答案 why**　询问做许多蛋糕的原因，用 why。

**39. 答案 illness**　形容词 serious 修饰名词 illness。

**40. 答案 of**　It is kind of sb. to do sth. 为固定结构。

**41. 答案 a**　so pleasant a dinner 为固定语序。

**42. 答案 inviting**　介词 for 后接动名词 inviting。

**43. 答案 saying**　现在分词 saying 作伴随状语。

**44. 答案 Happiness**　句首作主语用名词 Happiness。

**45. 答案 shared**　when shared 为省略的被动结构。
